# Business questions

## Main question

**How much does a user's consent decision change what a website loads, and who benefits from it?**

## For users: the cost of consent

1. **What does clicking "Accept all" cost the user in data, device resources and load time?**
   Tracking bytes and requests within the 30-second window, script execution time, and the share of tracking in total page weight, before and after consent.
2. **How many companies receive data about a visit after accepting, and how much of it is identity syncing?**
   Number of unique companies per site and the share of ID-syncing requests.
3. **Does "Reject all" actually stop tracking?**
   Tracking after a verified reject, by type: advertising, analytics, identity. Sites where identity syncing or analytics remain after rejecting are reported separately.

## For publishers and website owners

4. **Which types of websites carry the heaviest tracking load, and how big is the gap between categories?**
   News, portals and weather vs. e-commerce, government and health sites. This is the central comparison of the study.
5. **How concentrated is the tracking market: which companies are present on most sites and account for most of the data?**
   Reach and data volume by company: Google vs. the rest, and the role of ad exchanges and identity providers.
6. **How much does the consent banner itself weigh, and which consent platforms dominate the German market?**
   Bytes in the consent category and the distribution of consent management platforms (Sourcepoint, OneTrust, Usercentrics, consentmanager, etc.).

## For regulators and consumer protection

7. **How common is "pay or okay", and does it hide the reject option or remove it entirely?**
   Two groups seen in the pilot: reject is available only in the settings, or there is no reject without a subscription.
8. **How often does tracking start before the user makes any choice?**
   Tracker cookies and advertising or identity requests before any interaction, with a list of candidates for manual verification. Framed as indicators, not violations.

## Methodological question

9. **How much of tracking is visible from the browser at all?**
   Share of sites that block the crawler, and examples of server-side tracking. This is the limit of what can be measured from the outside.

## Main questions for the presentation

Questions 1, 3, 4 and 7. The others become additional dashboard sections.

## Out of scope

- **Legality** of specific practices: this is for lawyers and data protection authorities; the data only shows indicators.
- **What happens to the data on the servers** after it is sent.
- **Logged-in users and inner pages**: only home pages are measured, without logging in.
- **Publisher revenue**: there is no data on how much consent earns publishers.
